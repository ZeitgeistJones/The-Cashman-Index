# Math audit — August 2026

Two independent reviewers audited the index at commit `70e1468`. Every finding
below was confirmed **arithmetically by both**, against the same data, before it
was written down. The numbers are reproducible with:

```bash
python scripts/check_invariants.py
```

Nothing here is a matter of taste. The genuinely debatable calls are in
[Judgment calls](#judgment-calls-not-defects) at the bottom, and are deliberately
*not* enforced by the harness.

---

## Why two reviewers found different things

The two passes had different scopes, and the split turned out to matter:

- One audited the **aggregation layer** — `scoring.py`, `build_rankings.py`, the
  `build_*_index.py` builders, `weights.json`, `lib/lenses.ts`, and whether the
  About page's claims match the formulas.
- The other audited the **measurement layer** — `build_moves.py`, where
  `net_war_exchange` and `surplus_value` are computed in the first place.

Every trade metric in the aggregation layer is a sum over the measurement
layer's output. The most severe defect (#1) lives in the measurement layer and
was invisible from above: the aggregate numbers looked plausible. Neither pass
alone would have found the full set.

## The seven defects

### 1. Trade accounting is asymmetric — league net WAR is −3,769

The two sides of a trade use different horizons. WAR gained is counted only
while the player stays with the acquiring club; WAR lost is counted **everywhere
the departing player goes, forever**.

| | |
|---|---|
| WAR credited to buyers | 4,709 |
| WAR charged to sellers | 8,479 |
| Asymmetry | **1.80×** |
| League-wide sum | **−3,769** (must be ≈ 0) |

Trades are a closed market: one club's gain is another's loss, so the league
total must be about zero. Instead **all 30 clubs grade out as losing traders**,
mean −5.03 WAR/season. Every downstream trade metric — franchise trade rate, GM
trade rate, season construction, yearly resumes — inherits this bias.

`enrich_moves` already computes `war_after_exit`; it is never used. That is the
missing ingredient for a symmetric horizon.

**Invariant:** league sum ≈ 0, and charged/credited ≈ 1.0.

### 2. Payroll efficiency measures the calendar, not skill

A win cost roughly half as much in 2006 as in 2025:

| 2006 | 2016 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| 125.2 | 67.8 | 56.2 |

*(league mean wins per $100M)*

Raw wins/$100M therefore ranks executives largely by **when they worked**. This
is the single heaviest-weighted component (0.36 balanced):

```
corr(GM tenure midpoint, payroll_efficiency) = -0.53
corr(GM tenure midpoint, final composite)    = -0.37
```

Dave Littlefield — by reputation one of the weakest GMs of his era — posts
158.4 wins/$100M and ranks **15th of 87**. He is not efficient; he is early.

**Invariant:** |corr(tenure era, payroll efficiency)| ≈ 0.

### 3. A missing component scores better than a mediocre one

Absent draft or trade books are coerced to `0.0` and enter the peer z-score
population as real zeros. Because most GMs grade negative on trades:

```
GM trade_net_rate:  mean = -3.34,  pstdev = 3.84  ->  z(0.0) = +0.87
```

Knowing **nothing** about a GM scores them at +0.87 standard deviations —
comfortably above average. Ten GMs currently sit at exactly 0.0. The season
board renormalizes around missing components; the career boards do not, and the
UI promises blank ≠ zero.

> `pstdev`, not sample stdev — that is what `scoring.zscore` uses. Sample stdev
> gives 3.86 and a test asserting it will flap.

**Invariant:** a missing component is `null` and renormalized, never `0.0`.

### 4. "As of" resumes use present-day hindsight

`resume_through_date` says draft VOS and trade net "are also cut at as_of so
yearly/exit composites cannot credit picks or deals that had not happened yet."

That is true of **occurrence** and false of **outcome**. It filters which picks
and deals count by date, then sums `pick["vos"]` and `move["net_war_exchange"]`,
both computed from WAR as of the last rebuild. A GM's 2010 resume knows how his
2008 draft class turned out through 2026.

Season construction *does* horizon-cap WAR. Resumes do not.

**Invariant:** WAR inputs to an as-of resume are capped at that cutoff.

### 5. Three different clocks

```
weights.json          as_of 2026-08-02
build_rankings.py:47  AS_OF = dt.date(2026, 8, 1)   # hardcoded, ignores weights.json
build_trade_index.py:30  AS_OF = dt.date.today()    # drifts daily
```

Five `date.today()` reads across the builders feed scoring. Two rebuilds of the
same commit produce different numbers. (`datetime.now()` in `generated_at` is
fine — a build stamp is not a scoring input.)

**Invariant:** no `date.today()` in builders; one pinned date from `weights.json`.

### 6. Indexes disagree about which seasons exist

```
trade_index, draft_index          window [2006, 2026]   -> 21 seasons
franchise, gm, season, yearly     window [2006, 2025]   -> 20 seasons
```

Trade rates are per-season averages divided by 21 while the rankings consuming
them use 20, quietly scaling one component ~5% against the others.

**Invariant:** every index reports the same window.

### 7. Duplicate records inflate WAR

Non-trade moves key on the raw MLB API row id, so one signing reported under
several ids becomes several moves. In `moves.json`: **33 duplicate groups, 45
redundant rows** of 2,662. DJ LeMahieu's 2021-01-27 signing appears **six
times**; Gerrit Cole's three times at 21.41 WAR each.

Separately, 46 players are credited across multiple distinct moves — **498 WAR
double-counted**. Aaron Judge's 64.35 WAR is credited to his original signing,
his call-up, *and* 27.18 to his 2022 re-signing.

Dedup key: `(move_date, move_type, sorted acquired names, sorted sent names)`.
Keying on `move_id` or including `counterparty` gives a different count.

**Invariant:** no duplicate groups under that key.

### Also: small-sample shrink is applied inconsistently

`tenure_shrink` is called once, at `build_rankings.py:647` in `build_gm_index`.
The yearly leaderboard (line 811) and exit resumes (line 726) call
`composite_scores` bare. A two-season GM is damped on the career board and not
on the yearly board, from the same record.

## Judgment calls, not defects

These are real modelling choices. The harness does **not** enforce them, and
reasonable people land differently.

**Call-ups scored as acquisitions.** Promoting a prospect is graded like signing
a free agent, crediting his whole career against the league minimum. The current
"best Yankees move of the decade" is Aaron Judge's contract selection at
**+$315M surplus** — which says more about the 2013 draft than about any 2016
decision. There is a real argument for keeping it (deciding when to promote *is*
front-office work), but it should be a stated choice.

**Nominal weights are close to unrelated to actual influence.** Correlating each
component against the shipped franchise composite:

| Component | Weight | r with composite |
|---|---|---|
| `payroll_efficiency` | **0.36** | **+0.24** ← weakest |
| `playoff_depth_rate` | 0.12 | +0.68 |
| `draft_vos` | 0.11 | **+0.65** |

The heaviest weight has the least leverage, because it is negatively correlated
with the winning block and partly cancels. `draft_vos` at a third the weight has
nearly triple the influence. Whether to fix this by reweighting or by disclosing
it is a thesis decision.

**The winning components are one thing measured four times.** World Series ⇒
pennant ⇒ playoff depth are nested, and `win_pct ~ playoff_depth_rate` at
**r = +0.88**. Stacked, that is ~0.41 of the balanced composite and ~0.56 under
the October lens, presented as four independent axes.

**A top-payroll club cannot score well.** The Yankees rank **2nd of 30 in
winning** and **30th of 30 in wins-per-dollar**, landing 10th overall. That may
be exactly the intended argument — but as built, no front office can overcome
its own payroll, and the About page should say so either way.

**`draft_vos` is near-zero variance amplified by z-scoring.** Franchise spread is
sd 0.075 WAR/pick around a mean of 0.000 — centred at zero by construction,
since expected WAR is the mean of the same picks being graded. Z-scoring
rescales that noise to sd 1 and hands it 0.11–0.20 weight.

## Fix order

1. **Trade horizon symmetry** — upstream of every trade metric; nothing
   downstream is meaningful until this is right
2. **Missing ≠ zero** — changes who is on the board at all
3. **Pinned `as_of`** and one window — makes results reproducible enough to
   verify the rest
4. **Dedup**
5. **Era-normalize payroll** — needs a chosen method (within-season z is the
   conventional default); reshuffles the GM board on purpose
6. **As-of WAR caps** — larger pipeline change; interacts with #1
7. **Shrink parity** — one line, once someone picks the canonical behaviour

Steps 1–4 are mechanical with unambiguous pass/fail. 5 and 6 are owner
decisions about method, not bugs with one correct patch.
